Part B · Study notes

One-page summary

Read this first for the big picture, then use the missions to test yourself.

Mission 1

The nervous system

  • CNS = brain + spinal cord
  • PNS = nerves connecting the CNS to the body
  • Together they communicate and coordinate
Mission 2

Neural messages

  • Dendrites receive, axon carries
  • Neurotransmitters cross the synapse
  • Receptors on the next neuron receive them
Missions 3 and 4

Drugs and neurotransmission

  • Drugs can interfere with neurotransmission and affect receptors
  • Stimulants generally increase activity, depressants generally reduce it
  • Opioids reduce pain, can dangerously slow breathing at high doses
  • Cannabis can affect memory, attention, coordination
Mission 6

Over time

  • Tolerance: need more for the same effect
  • Dependence: the body adapts
  • Addiction: continued use despite harm
  • Learning, memory and behaviour can change

Sense, process, respond

PNS

Message in. Nerves carry information from the eyes, skin and other senses toward the CNS.

→
CNS

Process. The brain and spinal cord interpret the information and decide what to do.

→
PNS

Instruction out. Nerves carry the instruction to muscles and organs, so the body responds.

Mission 6

What Happens Over Time?

ToleranceDependenceAddictionBrain functionLearning, memory, behaviour
Main sources: NIDA, Drugs and the Brain; NIDA, Mind Matters.
Step 1

Repeated use

The brain keeps meeting the drug, and neural communication is repeatedly changed.

Step 2

Tolerance

The same amount has a weaker effect, so a person may need more to feel the same.

Step 3

Dependence

The body adapts to the drug. Stopping can cause unpleasant withdrawal symptoms.

Step 4

Addiction

Continued drug use despite harmful consequences, with lasting changes in brain function.

Learning, memory and behaviour

  • Drugs can change the brain areas that handle attention, memory and decision making.
  • This can make learning harder and change behaviour.
  • The brain keeps developing through the teenage years, so early drug use can matter more.
  • Help works. Treatment and support exist for dependence and addiction.

Reflection

Draft reflection. Edit it in your own words before submitting.

I chose three sources that approach the topic from different angles. NIDA's Drugs and the Brain comes from a research agency within the National Institutes of Health and explains how drugs change brain communication. The StatPearls entry on the synapse by Caire et al. (2023) is a peer-reviewed reference that gives the basic science of how neurons communicate. NIDA's Mind Matters is written for learners, so it helped me think about how to explain ideas in a student-friendly way and which activities could work.

For the learning materials I used a mission-based case investigation instead of separate chapters, because students can discover the ideas step by step and apply them to a case. Each mission pairs a multimedia element with an interactive task, such as clicking a diagram, ordering steps, matching drug types and answering scenario questions. This suits independent, asynchronous learning because students get immediate feedback. Before publishing, I would re-check that every source is current and keep the explanations general, because drug effects vary between people and situations.
